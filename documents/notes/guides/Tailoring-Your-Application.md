# Tailoring Your Application

Source: `Source/tailoring_Captions_English (Australia).txt` (DeakinTALENT video transcript).
Companion to `Reading-a-Job-Advertisement.md`.

---

## The core claim

Tailoring is what makes you stand out. **The whole application is tailored**, cover letter and
resume both. It takes a lot longer per application. Quality beats quantity, always.

## Tailoring the cover letter

- Written specifically for each job and employer.
- **Use the keywords from your research as the topic of each body paragraph.** One keyword, one
  paragraph.
- You cannot fit every keyword. Be selective and take only the most important or relevant.
- Structure for each paragraph: name the requirement, say how you developed or used that skill,
  support it with an example, then **link it back to how it helps you in the job**.
- If the ad lists a key accountability or task, refer to it directly.

Worked example from the transcript: if the research shows the employer wants someone with
experience working with adults who have complex needs, there must be a paragraph on exactly
that, with an example, linked back to the role.

## Tailoring the resume

- **The resume is never finished.** Tweak, edit and change it for every job and organisation.
- It is a good idea to keep multiple copies tailored to different industries and roles.
- The most important section to tailor is the **career profile / career summary**. Choose a few
  words that highlight your unique offering and fit the organisation and job.
- **Tailor the bullet points under each past role.** Do not list every task, only the relevant
  ones.
- This applies even to irrelevant jobs. If the research shows building relationships matters,
  highlight the tasks that show you have done that, rather than pointing at cleaning, food
  preparation, and opening and closing the store.

## How to find the keywords

Start with the position description or job advertisement. They vary from a short paragraph to a
downloadable document of many pages. Either way, print it or save it so you can highlight.

The most important information is in the sections on **tasks, duties or responsibilities**, and
the **requirements you need to meet**. These appear under many different headings and can be
obvious or vague, so you may have to search for them.

Read the **entire** ad, including any description of the company, its values and its mission
statement.

**Four-highlighter method:**

1. Colour one: the **essential requirements**. Often the qualification, but also experience or
   specific skills.
2. Colour two: **all the skills**. Transferable and soft skills such as communication and
   teamwork, and technical skills such as specific software or lab techniques.
3. Colour three: the **level of experience** required.
4. Colour four: the **company values and attributes**.

**Then choose your keywords:**

- You cannot include every skill mentioned, so decide which matter most.
- **A skill mentioned more than once is a keyword.** In the transcript's example, communication
  appears twice alongside collaborate, support and advise, so it is obviously important.
- **The most important skills are usually in the first few points.** Administration made the
  keyword list purely because it was in the first point.
- **Consider what you have evidence for.** It may be easier to demonstrate innovation and
  positivity than to demonstrate being dynamic. Pick the ones you can prove.

Also identify any **specific instructions**: what format the resume must be in, whether there are
extra questions to answer, and how to submit.

## Widen the research

**The organisation:** does its mission and values match yours? Is it a large corporation with
scope for promotion, or a small business with varied day to day work? What projects are they
working on now? How do they describe themselves and their clients? Start with their website,
then social media, LinkedIn, Google, and ask your network what they know.

**The industry and sector:** understand current issues facing the industry, plus competitors and
strategies.

Students often miss that one job can exist across many sectors. An accounting student assumes
finance and business, but could work in the finance department of a hospital, where the current
issues facing healthcare are completely different. Knowing which sector you are actually
applying into changes what you write.
