## Why deconstruct the ad

Applying for as many jobs as possible regardless of suitability is the common mistake.
Employers write ads specifically to filter for suitable candidates, so the ad tells you exactly
what they are looking for. Deconstructing it does two things: it tells you whether the role is
something you want and can do, and it gives you the raw material to tailor the application.

## What an ad will tell you

Regardless of length or complexity, expect some of:

- What the job involves
- Full time, part time, casual, or fixed term contract
- Salary or hourly rate
- Reporting relationship, meaning who your manager or supervisor would be
- Required skills, qualifications and experience
- Organisational values and team structure
- How to apply
- Who to contact for further information

## The process

1. **Print it, or copy it into a document and save it,** so you can highlight and make notes.
2. **Identify the purpose of the role.** The responsibilities section says what is expected of
   the employee. Expect five to ten bullet points. **The first three usually cover the primary
   purpose of the role**, which tells you their importance.
3. **Look at the organisational mission and values.** Compare how well they align with your own.
   This tells you whether it is an organisation you want to work for.
4. **Find the essential and desirable requirements.** You want to meet **at least 70 percent** of
   the requirements to apply. If you do not, this is probably not the right position. Instead,
   identify the skills gap you need to fill to apply for a role like it in future.
5. **Read the "how to apply" section** to clarify the procedure. Which documents are required
   beyond a resume and cover letter? **Following the specified process is essential. Not doing so
   may mean immediate rejection.**
6. **Tailor the resume and cover letter** to the skills, attributes and experience the employer
   listed. **The words you use need to match the words the employer is using.** If the ad says
   negotiation skills or highly motivated, you must include specific statements demonstrating
   how you used negotiation skills and how you sustained high motivation.
7. **Consider the tone.** It signals the company's culture. You can mirror it while staying
   strongly professional.
8. **Address the cover letter to the contact person listed in the ad.** Do not be afraid to
   contact them to learn more about the role or clarify questions.

## The caveat

Every advertisement is different. It is on you to adapt this to the ad in front of you and do
additional research where the ad is thin.
