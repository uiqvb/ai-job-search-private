## 1. Purpose

- The cover letter introduces the application and is **as important as the resume**. It adds
  context the resume cannot carry, and its job is to make the employer want to know more.
- It must be **written specifically for each position**.
- It can be **speculative**, expressing interest in an organisation with nothing advertised.
- Always send it with the resume unless told otherwise.

## 2. How an employer actually reads it

1. **Formatting and readability first.** If it is not laid out as a structured business letter,
   or is not easy to read, it will not be read in detail. It gets discarded.
2. **Then detail, for about 10 seconds.** While reading they ask:
   - Does this person meet the selection criteria and give specific examples?
   - Do they show strong interest in, and understanding of, the position and organisation?
   - Do they make a positive first impression and do I want to know more?
3. **Then against the resume.** The two must look and read like one application package.
   Employers look for alignment and consistency between them.

## 3. Research before writing

Understand the organisation and the industry it sits in:

- What specifically does the company do? Core products and services?
- Organisational structure? Key clients?
- How is it different from its competitors?
- What are its culture and values?

## 4. Structure

```
Your Name
Your Street Address
Your Suburb, State and Postcode
Your Phone Number
Your Email Address

Date

Contact's Name
Contact's Position Title
Organisation Name
Organisation's Street Address
Organisation's Suburb, City and Postcode

Greeting line,
Reference line
```

**Introduction.** Why are you writing? Brief greeting, the **full correct position title** and
**full correct organisation name**. State where you found the ad. Name any referral (with their
permission) or a networking event or expo where you met an employee. Optionally one or two lines
on why the opportunity interests you at a high level.

**Body (one to three paragraphs).** Two questions: *how are you a suitable candidate?* and
*why, specifically, this organisation?*

- Highlight your current degree and relevant qualifications. Consider expected completion date,
  significant awards, academic average.
- Describe in detail **two to three** relevant skills, experiences and qualities. Draw from paid
  employment, internships, volunteer work or extra-curriculars.
- Each one must align directly to the position description and be supported by **specific,
  robust examples and evidence**.
- Show you have researched the organisation and know its brand. Be specific, move beyond basics,
  and show interest in and understanding of their work.

**Closing.** Restate the value you offer and your alignment. Refer to attached documents the
employer asked for (resume, academic transcript). State your interest in an interview. Thank
them.

## 4a. The template's own instructions, block by block

`../Templates/Cover-Letter-Template.docx` carries its own placeholder instructions, and they
are more specific than the guide PDF. **They are normative. Audit every letter against this
list before it goes out.**

### Block 1, sender

```
Your First and Last Name
Your Street Address
Your Suburb, State and Postcode

Your Phone Number (format: 04XX XXX XXX)
Your Email Address
```

Note the phone format `04XX XXX XXX`, and that a **street address** is asked for here, unlike
the resume where it is optional.

### Block 2, date

`Date (format: Wednesday 1 April 2026)` — weekday, day, month, year. Not numeric.

### Block 3, recipient

```
Contact's Name
Contact's Position Title (if unknown use Recruitment Manager or HR Manager)
Organisation Name
Organisation's Street Address
Organisation's Suburb, City and Postcode
```

### Block 4, greeting and reference line

- `Dear Firstname Lastname,` or `Dear Recruitment Manager,` or `Dear HR Manager,` or
  `Dear Hiring Manager,`
- Reference line format: `RE: Graduate IT Analyst position, REF:1259789`. Include the
  employer's reference number when the ad gives one.

### Block 5, introduction

Every one of these:

- [ ] Brief greeting and an explanation of why you are writing
- [ ] The **full, correct position title**
- [ ] The **full, correct organisation name**
- [ ] Where you found the job opening, if advertised
- [ ] The name of anyone who referred you, with their permission
- [ ] A reference to meeting an employee at a networking event or expo, if that happened
- [ ] **One to two lines on why you are interested in the opportunity**

### Block 6, body paragraph one

- [ ] How you are a suitable candidate
- [ ] Your **current degree** and other relevant qualifications
- [ ] Your **expected completion date**
- [ ] Any **significant awards**
- [ ] Your **academic average** (e.g. distinction average)
- [ ] **Two to three** relevant skills, experiences and qualities, described in detail, drawn
      from paid employment, internships, volunteer work or extra-curricular activities
- [ ] Each one directly aligned to the position description, and supported with specific,
      concrete examples and evidence

### Block 7, body paragraph two

- [ ] Demonstrate you have researched the organisation and know its brand
- [ ] Describe **what attracted you to the organisation**, specifically, moving beyond basics
- [ ] Convey enthusiasm for the industry, and interest in and understanding of their work

### Block 8, closing

- [ ] Reiterate what you bring, and your alignment to the position and organisation
- [ ] Refer to any **attached documents** the employer requested (resume, academic transcript)
- [ ] State your **interest in attending an interview**
- [ ] **Thank** the employer for their consideration

### Block 9, sign-off

`Yours Sincerely`, `Kind Regards`, or `Yours Faithfully`, then your first and last name.
No e-signature needed.

> **Where a posting's own instructions conflict with this list, the posting wins.** If an ad
> demands specific numbered questions be answered, answer them first and fit the template's
> requirements around them. Both still have to be satisfied within one page.

## 5. Content rules that get letters rejected

| Rule | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Length | **Does not exceed one page.** |
| Emphasis | On what you can **offer**, not what you can gain. |
| Duplication | Must add context and depth to the resume, not repeat it. |
| Desperation | Never "willing to do anything", "open to all opportunities". |
| Tone | Positive, approachable, professional. Never "I hereby apply for", "Moreover, I", "My aforementioned skills", "This fine establishment". |
| Buzzwords | Do not overuse "expert", "passionate", "dynamic". Avoid "best candidate" and "perfect fit". |
| Acronyms | Only when necessary, and defined on first use. |
| Numbers | Write one to ten as words. "Delivered three presentations", not "Delivered 3 presentations". |
| Spelling | UK English, consistently. Watch capitalisation. |
| Sentences | Short, plain English, understandable on fast review. |
| Font | Same style and size as the resume. Calibri or Arial, 10 or 11pt. Single spacing, left justified. |
| Formatting | Same margins and bullet style as the resume. They must look like one package. |
| Never include | Icons, photos, headings, banners, borders, colour, tables, lists. Also no photo, date of birth, marital status, religion, nationality, or the words "Cover letter". |
| File name | `First name Surname Cover Letter.pdf`. |

Greeting: `Dear Firstname Lastname`. If unknown, `Dear Recruitment Manager` or `Dear HR Manager`.

## 6. The eight Deakin example letters, and what each demonstrates

All eight sit in `Source/Cover-Letter-Guide.pdf`, pages 5 to 14.

| Example | Scenario | The move worth stealing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ashley Siqqui (Telstra) | Graduate program | Names the assignment ("designing a survey to assess future students' needs"), then the result ("additional workshops and programs were implemented"). |
| Charlie Nguyen (NAB) | Graduate role | Opens the body by naming the employer's actual problem ("challenges exist with systems analysis within a large organisation like NAB") before offering the match. |
| Sasha Creedy (PGK) | Met a grad at an employer showcase | Names the person, the event, the month, and what specifically she said that captured interest. |
| Ali Beeza (Better Rehabilitation) | Referral | Leads with the referral in the first sentence, and what that person told them about the culture. |
| Reese Willner (Cardno) | PhD moving to industry | Frames the research background as transferable analytical skill, and is explicit about wanting to leave academia. |
| Alex Matthews (Edstart) | Advertised internship | Quantifies the extra-curricular ("15% increase in our membership base"). |
| Rae Sans (SBS) | **Speculative** internship | Opens "I am writing to explore whether an opportunity might exist", then proves genuine familiarity with a specific recent production. |
| Jamie Silver (Mecca) and Sam Holmes (Human Rights Collective) and Blair Hollogst (cafe) | Casual and part-time, including no experience | Transferable duties from unrelated work, plus one volunteering example with a named commendation. |
