# 05. The Students in the Pink Shirts

**Hook:** A year ago I was looking for the students in pink shirts because I needed their help.
This time I would like to be wearing one.

**Used in:** Deakin Student Experience Ambassador. It is the spine of that letter.

**Best for:** student-facing roles, ambassador and orientation work, anything where lived
experience of the user is the qualification.

---

## The story

- A year ago I walked into Orientation at Deakin with no idea where I belonged. I had recently
  arrived in Australia as an international student.
- Starting university is already strange. New people, classes, expectations, and the
  uncomfortable feeling that everyone else understands what is happening slightly better than
  you do. For me there was another layer. Deakin was new. Australia was new. Almost everything
  was new.
- I remember walking through Burwood during Orientation, overwhelmed by how alive it was. People
  everywhere, music, clubs, stalls, groups already laughing together. Part of me was excited.
  Another part was standing in the middle of it thinking, where exactly do I fit into this?
- That is one of the lonelier things about being new. You can be surrounded by thousands of
  people and still feel like the only person who does not know what they are doing.
- Then I met the students in the pink Deakin shirts.
- I cannot tell you a profound piece of advice one of them gave me. I do not remember one. What I
  remember is much simpler. They were easy to talk to. They smiled. They answered questions I was
  probably overthinking before I asked them. They spoke to me without making me feel like the new
  international student who did not know how anything worked.
- For a few minutes, I was just another Deakin student. That distinction mattered more than I
  understood at the time.
- They did not make the nerves disappear. I still had to build friendships, learn the campus and
  adjust to living in Australia. But they made the place feel smaller. A little more familiar.
  A little more like somewhere I could belong.
- Eventually it did become that. Over the following year I joined the Software Engineering Club,
  then the Data Science Club, served on committees, helped run events, hosted networking sessions
  and became familiar with a campus that had once felt enormous.

## The closing move

The letter ends by projecting forward rather than summarising:

> There will be thousands of students moving through Burwood during Orientation. Some will arrive
> surrounded by friends. Some will be confident enough to talk to everyone. Some will only need
> to know where their next session is. And somewhere among them will be another student who has
> just arrived in Australia, knows almost nobody, and is quietly wondering whether this enormous
> new place will ever feel like theirs.
>
> I cannot make that adjustment for them. The ambassadors I met could not make it for me either.
> But they could make the first day easier.

## What it evidences

- Genuine, specific motivation for a student-facing role
- Empathy grounded in a real experience rather than asserted
- Understanding of what the role actually delivers, which is not information, it is lowering the
  cost of asking

## Length variants

**One line.** I am applying for this because a year ago I was the overwhelmed international
student the role exists for, and the ambassadors in pink shirts made Deakin feel smaller for me.

**Short.** A year ago I arrived at Orientation as a new international student with no idea where
I belonged, and the thing that helped was not advice, it was students in pink shirts who were
easy to talk to and spoke to me like I was already one of them. I am applying because I remember
very clearly what it felt like to be the student this role exists for.

## Facts and verification

- International student, arrived in Australia, first Orientation roughly one year before
  August 2026. Check the exact intake date before writing "a year ago" in a new letter.
- Pink shirts: the Deakin student ambassador uniform as I saw it at Orientation. If the uniform
  colour has changed, cut the detail rather than risk it.
- Now selected as a Student Ambassador with Deakin Future Students for Open Day. This changes
  the ending: the letter can now say the loop closed.

## Do not say

- Any version that makes Deakin sound isolating overall. The letter is careful to credit the
  place for eventually becoming home. Keep that balance.
