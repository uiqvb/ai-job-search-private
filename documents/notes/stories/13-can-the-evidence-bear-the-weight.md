# 13. Can the Evidence Bear the Weight

**Hook:** My contribution to the hackathon was the unglamorous half: finding out whether the data
could support the thing we wanted to build, before the team built it.

**Used in:** CSIRO (Technology 14).

**Best for:** research roles, data science adjacent work, feasibility and scoping, anything where
the honest description of my role is not "I built it".

---

## The story

- ParkSense: a Melbourne CBD parking availability predictor, built for the Deakin Data Science
  Club hackathon over the summer trimester. It won first place.
- **I originated the concept.**
- My main contribution was establishing whether the public sensor feed we wanted to use carried
  enough signal to support prediction at all, before the team committed to building on it.
- Framed in the CSIRO letter as: asking whether the evidence can bear the weight of the
  capability, which is work I have already found I enjoy.

## Why this framing is the right one

This is a story where the modest version is stronger than the ambitious one. Claiming the build
would collapse under a single technical question, and the team lead has published a public
write-up crediting himself with the endpoints, the frontend, the pipeline and the model.
Claiming the **viability research** is both true and, for a research application, more relevant
than claiming the code would have been.

## What it evidences

- Originating an idea and getting a team to build it
- Willingness to do the necessary unglamorous work
- Understanding that a capability is only as good as the evidence underneath it

## Length variants

**One line.** I originated ParkSense and ran the early data-viability research, assessing whether
the public sensor feed could support prediction at all before the team committed to it.

**Short.** On a hackathon project I originated, my main contribution was the unglamorous half of
the problem: establishing whether the public sensor feed we wanted to use carried enough signal
to support prediction at all, before the team committed to building on it. Asking whether the
evidence can bear the weight of the capability is work I have already found I enjoy.
