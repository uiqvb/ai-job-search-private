# 06. The Student Who Hovers

**Hook:** I stopped trying to give people the perfect pitch and started asking what they studied.

**Used in:** Deakin Student Experience Ambassador.

**Best for:** engagement, outreach, sales and stall work, customer-facing roles, anything about
approachability or initiating contact.

---

## The story

- At Market Day I began noticing the students who hovered around our stall without approaching.
  They would walk past once, look over, keep going, and eventually circle back.
- I recognised that hesitation, because it had been mine (story 05).
- So I stopped thinking about how to give them the perfect pitch. I started asking what they
  studied.
- Often that was enough. They would tell me their degree, I would ask how they were finding it,
  one question became another, and someone who had been standing several metres away wondering
  whether to approach was having a normal conversation with another student.

## The insight it carries

> I used to think helping someone meant having the information they needed. Now I think it often
> begins earlier than that. Sometimes helping someone means noticing that they have a question
> before they are comfortable asking it. Sometimes it means starting the conversation for them.

This is the transferable part. It generalises well beyond a stall.

## What it evidences

- Reading a room and noticing non-obvious signals
- Lowering the barrier to contact rather than raising the quality of the pitch
- Converting hesitation into engagement, with a measurable outcome attached (sign-ups)

## Length variants

**One line.** At Market Day I learned to notice the students hovering near the stall without
approaching, and that asking what they studied worked better than any pitch.

**Short.** Fronting club stalls at Market Day, I noticed students who would walk past, look over,
and circle back without approaching. Rather than sharpen the pitch, I started asking what they
studied. That was usually enough to turn someone standing several metres away into a normal
conversation, and contributed to more than 100 new member sign-ups.
