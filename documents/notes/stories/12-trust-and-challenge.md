# 12. The Unit Chair Who Challenged Me Instead of Approving Me

**Hook:** He did not shut the idea down, and he did not just tell me it was great. He made sure I
understood the risk, then let me choose.

**Used in:** Suncorp, as the answer to why this organisation.

**Best for:** culture fit, "what environment do you do your best work in", questions about
feedback, mentorship, and psychological safety.

---

## The story

- I brought my unit chair an idea that would make two important projects substantially harder
  (story 01).
- He did not shut it down. He also did not simply tell me it was a great idea and leave it there.
- He listened to what I wanted to do, challenged my thinking, made sure I understood the risks,
  and then let me choose my own path.
- He gave me room to make the project mine while still pushing me to do more with it.

## What changed in me

That combination of trust and challenge brought out something I had not seen in first year. I
became more willing to experiment, more comfortable defending an idea, more open to having that
idea questioned, and more determined to work through problems I did not yet know how to solve.

## Why it is useful

Most "culture fit" paragraphs assert a preference. This one gives evidence that the preference
has already been tested, and describes what the environment produced. It also lets you say what
you want from an employer without it reading as a list of demands:

> I want to work with people who will listen when I have an idea, challenge it when it needs
> challenging, and trust me to take ownership of what comes next. I want to encounter systems I
> do not understand yet, get things wrong, ask questions, defend my thinking when I believe in
> it, and change my mind when someone shows me something better.

## How Suncorp connected it back

The letter pairs it with something specific found in their own material: an intern named Imaan
describing being given hands-on experience and feeling her opinions and fresh perspective were
valued, rather than observing from the sidelines. That is the research step from
`../Guides/Tailoring-Your-Application.md` done properly. **Whenever this story is reused, replace
that reference with something equally specific from the new organisation.** Without it the
paragraph is generic.

## What it evidences

- Responds well to challenge rather than to praise
- Takes ownership when given room
- Knows what conditions bring out their best work, and can say why

  decision, and the risk, were mine. That is the point of the story.
