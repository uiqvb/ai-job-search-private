# 10. Naming the Gap

**Hook:** I have not used ROS or ROS2, and I am not going to dress that up.

**Used in:** CSIRO (both versions). It is the paragraph most likely to have been the reason the
letter was taken seriously.

**Best for:** any application where I clearly miss a listed requirement. This is a reusable
**move**, not just a fact about ROS.

---

## The move

Four steps, in this order:

1. **Name the gap plainly, and say you are not going to dress it up.** No hedging verbs, no
   "limited exposure to".
2. **Say why it matters more in one place than another.** In the CSIRO letter: it matters more
   for Technology 14 than for Technology 11. This proves you understand the roles well enough to
   know where your own gap bites.
3. **Offer the substrate underneath it, concretely.** Not a claim of transferability, an actual
   list: MQTT publish and subscribe topics carrying JSON telemetry on a fixed interval between a
   microcontroller and a Python supervisor, that telemetry logged, and the system debugged from
   the record rather than from the hardware. Plus the brownout story (09). Plus working in both
   C++ and Python.
4. **State what you will have done about it by the time you arrive.** "I would rather name this
   now and arrive in November having worked through the ROS2 basics in my own time than overstate
   it here and have you find out in week one."

## Why it works

It converts a disqualifier into evidence of self-assessment, and it removes the interviewer's
best trap question by asking it first. The alternative, burying the gap, means the letter has to
be true only until someone checks.

## What it evidences

- Honest calibration of your own ability
- Understanding of the role deep enough to locate your own weakness within it
- A plan, with a date attached

## Reusable template

> I should be straightforward about one gap, because it matters more for [X] than for [Y]. I have
> not [the gap], and I am not going to dress that up. What I do have is the substrate underneath
> it: [two or three concrete, verifiable things]. I would rather name this now and arrive in
> [month] having worked through the basics in my own time than overstate it here and have you
> find out in week one.

## Facts and verification

- **No ROS or ROS2 experience.** Still true as at August 2026. If that changes, this file and the
  CSIRO letters both need updating, and the change is worth mentioning proactively to CSIRO if
  the application is still live.
- Works in both C++ and Python: true, see the embedded and hackathon entries in `../CLAUDE.md`.
- The stated plan was to work through ROS2 basics before November 2026. **This is a commitment
  that was put in writing to a selection panel.** Treat it as a deadline, not a flourish.

## Other gaps this move could be applied to

- No commercial or industry software experience yet. All projects are university, club or
  volunteer.
- No formal research methods training. Already handled this way in the CSIRO Final letter, which
  names methodology, documentation, analysis, reproducibility and experimental design as things
  only begun to be experienced.
