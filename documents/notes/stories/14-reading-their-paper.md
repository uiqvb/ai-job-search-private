# 14. Reading Their Paper Before Applying

**Hook:** The result I found most useful was not the headline finding. It was the one that
complicates it.

**Used in:** CSIRO Technology 11.

**Best for:** research applications, any organisation with published work, and as a template for
the "why specifically this organisation" paragraph that `../Guides/Cover-Letter-Guide.md`
demands.

---

## The move

Read something the team actually published. Then, rather than praising it, engage with a
secondary result and say what it implies for the work you would be doing.

## The worked example

Paper: **"What did the Robot do in my Absence?"**

- The headline result is that storyboards came out ahead overall. That is not the useful part.
- The useful parts: storyboards were **weaker on event-related questions than on object-related
  ones**, and query-driven summaries **bought accuracy at the cost of time**.
- What that implies: there may be no single winning representation, only representations matched
  to the kind of error an operator is being asked to catch.
- Then the extension to their actual brief: moving from "what did the robot do" to "what is the
  robot about to do" makes it **harder rather than easier**, because a proposed plan has no
  ground truth yet for the operator to check against, and a heterogeneous team means the reviewer
  also has to hold each robot's differing skills and limits in their head while they read it.

## Why it works

Three things happen at once. It proves the reading is real, because a summary could not produce
that observation. It shows you can hold a result and its limits at the same time. And it ends
pointing at their open problem rather than at yourself.

## Reusable template

> In preparing this application I read [specific thing]. The result I found most useful was not
> [the headline], but that [the complicating secondary finding]. That is a warning that
> [implication]. Extending that to [what the role actually asks] seems to me to make it harder
> rather than easier, because [reason one] and [reason two]. Those are the questions I would like
> to spend [duration] on.

This template works for a paper, a product decision, an engineering blog post, or an annual
report. The company research step in `../Guides/Tailoring-Your-Application.md` is the input.

## What it evidences

- Genuine preparation, at a depth that cannot be faked
- Critical reading rather than agreement
- Framing your interest as a question rather than as enthusiasm

## Facts and verification

- The paper is cited by title only in the letter. **Have the full citation and authors to hand
  before any interview**, and be ready to be asked what else was in it.
- The CSIRO letter states the Technology 11 brief includes a user study where participants review
  plans containing deliberately introduced errors. That comes from the project brief in
  `../Job_Ads/103682_Technology_Vacation_Studentships_2026.xlsx`. Recheck the brief before
  reusing any description of it.

## Do not say

- That I have read the team's work broadly, or "the literature". One paper was read carefully.
  Claiming more invites a question with a bad answer.
