# 02. Taking Ownership of the Degree

**Hook:** I was pushed into software engineering, coasted through first year, and then decided to
find out what I could actually do.

**Used in:** Asta, Deloitte, Suncorp. Always as the setup for story 01.

**Best for:** self-awareness, motivation, growth, "tell us about yourself", explaining an
imperfect first-year transcript before someone asks.

---

## The story

- Software engineering was a path encouraged by my parents and my brother. I did not choose it.
  For most of first year I treated university as something to get through, one foot in and one
  foot out.
- I still finished first year with a Distinction average. That should have made me happy. It
  bothered me, because I knew how little I had tried.
- One Credit on the transcript frustrated me more than anything else. Not because it was a bad
  mark, but because I had earned exactly what I put in, which was not much.
- Second year, I decided to change. At first the motivation was pride. I wanted better marks and
  I wanted to prove something to people who had watched me coast.
- It may not have been the most mature reason to change, but it led somewhere unexpected. I began
  to genuinely enjoy technology.
- I started staying ahead of deadlines, engaging with teaching staff, and putting real effort
  into understanding what I was building.
- I stopped asking what I needed to do to finish an assignment and started asking what else I
  could make it do. Assignments became opportunities to test ideas.
- Somewhere in there, software engineering stopped being the degree my family wanted me to do.
  It became mine.

## What it evidences

- Honest self-assessment without excuses
- Intrinsic motivation replacing external motivation, and the ability to see the difference
- A concrete, dated behaviour change rather than a claim about character

## Length variants

**One line.** I started this degree because my family pushed me into it and I coasted through
first year. In second year I decided to actually try, and somewhere in that I started enjoying
it enough to make my own work harder.

**Short.** I was basically pushed into software engineering. For most of first year I did the
minimum, and while I finished with a Distinction average it bothered me because I knew how little
I had tried. In second year I changed that, at first out of pride, then because I actually
started enjoying it. I stopped asking what I needed to do to finish an assignment and started
asking what else I could make it do.

**Full.** `../Archive/Cover_Letters/Manit_Khera_Cover_Letter_Suncorp.docx` has the strongest
version. It is written in very short lines and lands harder for it.

## Why it works, and the risk

It works because it earns the story that follows. Without it, the Frankenstein project is just a
student who did extra work. With it, the project is evidence of a change.

The risk is real: you are volunteering a weakness. Only run this when the pairing with story 01
is intact, and never in a resume or a short-form application. It also puts a Credit in front of
someone who may not have asked. Suncorp and Deloitte both got the full version because both ask
culture-fit questions where the change is the point.

## Facts and verification

- First year: Distinction average, with one Credit. Currently a High Distinction average.
  Confirm current WAM against the transcript before printing any figure.
- Perfect scores referenced elsewhere: 100 in Backend Development, 100 in Data Structures and
  Algorithms, 93 in Embedded Systems.

## Do not say

- Anything that blames family. The letters are careful here, and the phrasing "a path encouraged
  by my parents and brother" is deliberately neutral. Keep it that way.
- "I was forced" in a formal or conservative context. Suncorp got "I was basically forced into
  software engineering" and it worked because the letter immediately takes responsibility. Do
  not lift that line without the paragraphs that follow it.
