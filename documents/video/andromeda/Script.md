# Intro video: talking head script

For Carina Parisella, Andromeda Robotics. About 90 seconds, 8 sections.
Built on the same order as Andromeda's own team videos (Luke, Audio; Will Dean, Autonomy;
the office fit-out tour), so it feels like one of theirs.

Facts check: every line is on record in CLAUDE.md or Interview Prep.md. The Wi-Fi recovery is a
"what if" he built for, never something that happened to him.

---

## FILMING VERSION (about 90 seconds, 230 words). Film this one.

1. **Hook**, to camera: "Ever been told you can't do something, and then gone and done it just to prove a point?"
2. **Who I am**, to camera: "Hey, I'm Manit, a software engineering student at Deakin, and I build robots that run on software I write."
3. **What I built**, next to the robot: "My unit chair said I couldn't build this. So I did. This is my robot, and the backend that controls it. The server does all the thinking, the robot just does what it's told."
4. **The challenge**, next to the robot: "One of the challenges with that is, what if it loses Wi-Fi mid-job? So every command comes with its own undo. Seven seconds of silence, and it backs itself out."
5. **Show the screen**, at the laptop: "What you're looking at here is the dashboard. I can send it jobs, or just drive it around like an RC car."
6. **When it broke**, battery pile in shot: "It wasn't always smooth. For ages it only worked on fresh batteries. I kept buying more before the logs showed the real problem: the voltage converter. So now I own a lot of ninety percent full batteries."
7. **Why Andromeda**, to camera, slower and quieter than the rest:
   "So why Andromeda?
   My grandfather was pretty lonely at the end of his life.
   I was with him for his last few months. Then I moved to Melbourne for uni.
   He passed two days after I landed.
   Then I saw a video of Abi dancing with an old lady.
   And I thought of him straight away.
   And of course my brain went 'what if'.
   What if someone's family could log in and be Abi for a bit?
   Their face on her screen. Dancing with their grandparent from the other side of the world.
   I don't know if that's possible yet.
   But that's the kind of robot I want to help build.
   And I want to learn how from the people who built her."

   Source: told by Manit 2026-10-18. Keep the family conflict and property dispute OUT; private,
   and not needed. The telepresence idea is his own "what if", pitched humbly, not as a feature
   request.
8. **Close**, to camera, small smile, energy back up:
   "So yeah. I'm Manit.
   I build things people tell me I can't.
   I'd love to build the next one with you.
   Thanks for watching."
   Hold 2 seconds after the last word; the robot drives into frame here in the edit.

The longer version below is kept for reference.

## 1. Hook (about 5 seconds)

*Their move: open on a question.*

> Ever been told you can't do something, and then gone and done it just to prove a point?

## 2. Who I am (about 8 seconds)

*Their move: "Hey, I'm Luke, a robotics engineer for..."*

> Hey, I'm Manit, a software engineering student at Deakin, and I build robots that are run from
> software I write.

## 3. What I built (about 12 seconds)

*Their move: "I'm designing and implementing..."*

> I built a little robot, and a backend that controls it. The server does all the thinking, and
> the robot just does what it's told. It started because my unit chair said "nah, you can't do
> that". So I did.

## 4. The challenge (about 15 seconds)

*Their move: "One of the challenges with that is..."*

> One of the challenges with that is, what if the robot loses Wi-Fi halfway through a job? So I
> made every command come with its own undo. If it goes quiet for seven seconds, it backs itself
> out on its own.

## 5. Show the screen (about 10 seconds)

*Their move: "What I'm looking at here is..." Have the dashboard open and point at it.*

> What you're looking at here is the dashboard. I can send it jobs, or just drive it around with
> the keyboard like an RC car.

## 6. When it broke (about 12 seconds)

*Your extra section. Deadpan.*

> It wasn't always smooth. For ages it only worked on fresh batteries, so I kept buying new ones
> before I found the real problem in its logs: the voltage converter. So now I own a lot of
> ninety percent full batteries.

## 7. Why Andromeda (about 18 seconds)

*Their move: "The reason why I love working at Andromeda is..." then "On top of that..."*

> Most robots are built to move boxes. Abi's built to remember your grandkids' names and blow
> bubbles with you. That's why Andromeda.
>
> And honestly? My robot follows a line on the floor. Abi has to find her way around a care home
> she's never seen, with people and wheelchairs everywhere, and keep going when the Wi-Fi drops.
> That gap, between my robot and Abi, is exactly what I want to learn. And I'd rather learn it
> from the people who are already doing it.

Delivery: smile on "blow bubbles". Slow down and drop the energy slightly on "And honestly?",
it should sound like an admission, not a pitch. Pick it back up on the last line.

Why it is built this way: the first two sentences are the hook (a contrast nobody else in the
pile will use). The admission is the honest part, and it makes you more believable, not less:
the panel can see your robot is line-following, so naming the gap first means they don't have to.
Facts: Abi remembers family details (Interview Prep.md), bubbles are "one of her most favourite
features" (office tour video), unmapped homes with people and wheelchairs is Will's own problem,
unreliable care-home Wi-Fi is the obstacle Grace Brown names.

## 8. Where I come from, and close (about 10 seconds)

*Their move: "I've worked in audio roles and software roles... the nice combination of both."*

> I've done backend and I've done embedded, and robots are where the two meet. So yeah, I'm
> Manit. Thanks for watching.

## How to film it

- **One section per take.** Film section 1 two or three times, then section 2, and so on. Clap
  once before each take. Their videos are made of short cuts, so yours can be too.
- **Sections 1, 2, 7 and 8:** sitting or standing, straight to camera, like Luke at his desk.
- **Sections 3 and 4:** standing next to the robot, like Will in the workshop.
- **Section 5:** sitting at the laptop with the dashboard open, phone over your shoulder, like
  Will's map shot.
- **Section 6:** battery pile in shot if you can.
- Vertical, phone at eye level, face a window, quiet room, look at the lens.
- **Don't memorise the wording.** Read the section, put the phone down, say it your way.
- Slightly more energy than feels natural.

## Timing

About 90 seconds. If it runs long, shorten section 4 to one sentence.
